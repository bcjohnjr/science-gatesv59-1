# Negative-emissions novelty benchmark — V59.1

## Bottom line

**Carbon-sink weakening and sink-to-source reversal under net-negative CO2 emissions are not novel discoveries.**
They are established in peer-reviewed Earth-system literature and summarized in IPCC AR6.

Therefore the manuscript must not claim novelty for discovering sink reversal itself.

## Established prior work

### Jones et al. (2016), Environmental Research Letters
Earth-system models showed strong weakening and potential reversal of land and ocean sinks under low/negative-emissions pathways, and explicitly concluded that weakened natural sinks increase the amount of negative emissions required for a target.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/11/9/095012

### Zickfeld et al. (2021), Nature Climate Change
Demonstrated asymmetry between positive and negative CO2 emissions: an emission raises atmospheric CO2 more effectively than an equal removal lowers it, with asymmetry increasing with pulse magnitude.

Article: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-021-01061-2

### IPCC AR6 WGI Chapter 5
AR6 states that under scenarios with large net-negative CO2 emissions and rapidly declining concentrations, both land and ocean can switch from sinks to transient sources, with timing/magnitude dependent on removal history.

https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-5/
https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/figures/chapter-5/figure-5-33/

### Later literature
Recent studies continue to examine lag, hysteresis and source/sink reversals under negative emissions rather than treating them as unknown phenomena.

Examples:
https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2024EF004891
https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/acab1a

## Sea-level irreversibility is also established

Long sea-level lag after mitigation/CDR is not novel by itself. Prior work and AR6 already establish continuing sea-level rise after emissions and/or surface warming decline.

Examples:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-02985-8
https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2020GL088507
https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-9/

## What could still be novel here

The potentially publishable contribution is narrower:

1. Apply a **specific finance-constrained, globally deployed removal trajectory** to calibrated external carbon-cycle models.
2. Quantify how much the resulting state-dependent sink response changes the atmospheric drawdown relative to the internal reduced-complexity assumption.
3. **Invert the external model** to obtain the extra removal trajectory required for a defined atmospheric target.
4. Test whether the proposed financing architecture can actually fund that externally required removal schedule.
5. Show that even where surface warming declines, slow variables such as sea level remain displaced.

The inverse solve is therefore the key novelty discriminator. Without it, the package mainly reproduces known qualitative negative-emissions carbon-cycle behavior on a new pathway.

## Manuscript claim boundary

Do not claim:
- discovery of sink reversal;
- discovery that negative emissions are asymmetric with positive emissions;
- discovery that sea level lags surface temperature.

Potentially claim, if the inverse solve supports it:
- pathway-specific quantification of the **additional removal burden created by state-dependent sink response** under a fully specified finance/deployment architecture;
- whether that externally calculated burden is financeable and physically deployable.
